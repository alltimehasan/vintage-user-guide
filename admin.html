Logging in

Copy link

The shop team logs in on the same page as customers. The website knows who is staff and opens the admin for you.

  1. Go to the website and choose Account → Log in in the top menu, or add /login to the shop's web address.
  2. Enter your Email address and Password, then press Log in.
  3. If two-factor login is switched on for you, enter the 6-digit code from your authenticator app.
  4. You arrive on the Dashboard. The admin's address is the shop's address followed by /admin.

Your first login

When an admin invites you, you get an email called “Set your password for the Vintage Warehouse Glasgow admin”. Press Set your password in it and choose a password. The link works for 24 hours. If it has run out, use Forgot your password? on the login page to get a new one.

Password rules. At least 12 characters, with upper and lower case letters, a number and a symbol. The website also refuses passwords that have appeared in known data leaks, so pick something unique. A password manager makes this easy.

Forgotten your password?

  1. On the login page, press Forgot your password?.
  2. Enter your email address and press Email password reset link.
  3. Open the email “Reset your password” and press Reset Password. The email says the link expires in 1440 minutes, which is 24 hours.
  4. Choose a new password, then log in with it. Any other computer still logged in to your account is logged out.

You can ask for a new link once a minute. After 5 wrong passwords in a minute the login page makes you wait a little before trying again.

Passkeys and two-factor login

If you have added a passkey (see Your own login), press Sign in with a passkey under the login form and confirm with your fingerprint, face or device PIN. Two-factor login asks for a 6-digit code after your password; if you lose your phone, choose “or you can login using a recovery code” and enter one of your recovery codes.

Logging out

Open the menu with your name at the bottom of the sidebar and choose Log out. Always log out on a shared computer.

Finding your way around

Copy link

The menu down the left side (the sidebar) has everything. You only see the parts your role allows.

  • Shop: Dashboard, Products, Orders, Customers, Invoices and Messages. The amber number beside Orders is how many orders are waiting to be dispatched; the red number beside Messages is how many are unread.
  • Content: Pages.
  • SEO: Overview, Redirects and SEO settings.
  • Settings: Business, Shop and Users.
  • At the bottom: View website (opens the shop in a new tab), the light / dark / automatic switch, and your name, which opens Settings and Log out.

Who can do what

Every staff account has one of three roles. An admin chooses it when inviting someone and can change it later (Staff accounts).

Products, Orders, Customers, Invoices, MessagesYesYesNo

Nothing is lost by accident. When you change a form, an amber Unsaved changes label appears beside the Save button. If you try to leave by the sidebar or close the tab, the website asks first. (The browser's Back button is not caught, so press Save before going back.)

When you are logged in and browse the public website, the Account menu shows Shop admin, which brings you straight back here.

Orders and sales

Admins and shop managers see a chart of paid orders. Choose the time range with the calendar button at the top right:

  • Last 24 hours, Today, Yesterday: one bar per hour.
  • Last 7 days (the default), Last 30 days, This month, Last month: one bar per day.
  • Last 12 months: one bar per month.

Above the chart, three boxes show the number of Orders, the Sales and the Average order. Under Orders and Sales a line compares with the period before, for example “3 more than the 7 days before” in green or “£40.00 less than the day before” in red. A period that is still going on (Today, This month) is compared fairly: Today is compared with yesterday at this time, not the whole of yesterday.

  • Switch between Orders and Sales at the top right of the chart.
  • Show as table lists every hour, day or month with its figures.
  • Only paid orders count, on the day and time they were paid. Sales include VAT and delivery, less any refunds.

Orders

Copy link

Every checkout appears here, from the moment a customer presses Pay now. Your job is to pack the paid ones and mark them dispatched.

How an order works

  1. The customer presses Pay now in their basket. The order appears as Pending and its bags are held for them for 35 minutes, so nobody else can buy them meanwhile.
  2. They pay on Stripe's secure payment page. The order becomes Paid, gets its invoice number (0001, 0002…) and the stock goes down.
  3. The emails go out by themselves: the customer gets their order confirmation with the invoice attached; the shop's notification address and every staff account (admins, shop managers and SEO managers) get “New online order …” with a button to open it; and every staff account also gets a copy of the customer's confirmation, with Copy: at the start of its subject.
  4. You pack the bags, send the parcel and press Mark fulfilled. The customer's account then shows the order as Dispatched.

If the customer never pays, the order becomes Cancelled or Expired by itself and the bags go back on sale. You never need to tidy those up.

What each status means

StatusMeaningAnything to do?
PendingThe customer is on the payment page. The bags are held for 35 minutes (up to 7 days for a slow bank payment).No. Wait.
PaidMoney taken, invoice issued, emails sent.Yes: pack and dispatch.
FulfilledYou marked it dispatched. The customer sees “Dispatched”.No.
CancelledThe customer left the payment page, started again or logged out. Nothing was charged.No.
ExpiredThe payment page timed out unpaid, or a slow payment failed. Nothing was charged.No.
RefundedThe whole amount was refunded in Stripe.No.

Two extra labels can appear beside the status: Flagged (check before dispatch, see below) and Part refunded (some money was returned).

Finding an order

  • Use To dispatch to see only the orders you need to pack, and Flagged for those to check first.
  • Search by the customer's name, email or the invoice number.
  • Paid orders are shown by invoice number (“Invoice 0068”); unpaid ones by the end of their order code.
  • Press the eye button at the end of a row to open the order.

Dispatching an order

  1. Open Orders and choose To dispatch.
  2. Open the order. Check the Items and the Delivery address.
  3. If you want a paper copy for the parcel, press Download invoice and print it.
  4. Pack and send the parcel.
  5. Press Mark fulfilled, then Mark fulfilled again in the box that asks you to confirm.

Two things to know. Marking an order fulfilled cannot be undone, so do it only once the parcel has gone. And it does not email the customer: their account simply shows “Dispatched” with the date. To send a tracking number, email the customer yourself (their email address on the order page is a link).

Flagged orders: check before dispatch

Very occasionally a payment arrives that needs a human eye. The order is still paid and invoiced, but it gets a red Flagged label, a box saying “Check this order before dispatch”, and a dashboard warning. It happens when:

  • the customer paid after their checkout had already closed,
  • Stripe reports a different amount from the order total, or
  • there was not enough stock left when the payment arrived (two people paid for the last bag at the same moment).
  1. Read the reason in Admin notes.
  2. Press Open the payment in Stripe and check the payment there.
  3. If everything is fine, dispatch as normal. Marking it fulfilled clears the flag.
  4. If a bag is missing, refund the customer in Stripe (how) and email them to explain.

Resending the confirmation or remaking the invoice

The … button beside Download invoice has two more actions:

  • Resend confirmation sends the customer their confirmation email and invoice again (with the copy for the staff), and the shop team a fresh copy of the order notification. Use it when a customer says they never received it (ask them to check their spam folder too).
  • Regenerate PDF makes the invoice file again from the details saved when the order was paid. You will rarely need it.

Admin notes

Each order has a notes box that only staff can see. Write anything useful there (a tracking number, a phone call with the customer) and press Save notes. The website also writes into it when it flags an order.

The Customer box links to the customer's account with Their orders, which lists everything they have bought.

Refunds

Copy link

Refunds are made in Stripe, the payment company. The website notices by itself and updates the order.

  1. Open the order in the admin. In the Stripe box, press the Payment code (it starts with pi_). Stripe's website opens on that payment. Log in to Stripe if asked.
  2. In Stripe, press Refund. Choose the full amount, or type a smaller amount for a partial refund, and confirm.
  3. Within a few moments the order in the admin shows Refunded (full refund) or a Part refunded label, with the refunded amount in red under the total. The customer's account shows it too.
  4. Email the customer to let them know. The website does not send a refund email.

Returned bags. A refund does not put the bag back in stock. If a returned bag can be sold again, raise its Stock on the product page.

Customers can cancel within 14 days of receiving their order. They do it by email, the contact form or the cancellation form on the website; there is no cancel button in their account. Follow your Delivery and returns page for what to refund and when.

Invoices

Copy link

Every paid order gets one numbered VAT invoice, made automatically. Invoices are never changed or deleted.

  • Invoice numbers run 0001, 0002, 0003… with no gaps.
  • Search by number (“12” or “0012”) or the customer.
  • An invoice keeps the business details from the day it was paid. Changing Settings → Business later affects new invoices only.
  • The customer receives their invoice as a PDF with the order confirmation, and can open it again from their account.
7

Messages

Copy link

Messages sent through the website's contact form. Each one is also emailed to the shop's notification address and to every staff account.

  1. Open Messages and press Open on a message. Opening it marks it as read.
  2. Press Reply by email. Your email program opens with the customer's address and the subject filled in.
  3. Use Mark as unread if you want to come back to it, or Delete to remove it for good.

Quicker still: reply straight to the “New message from the website contact form” email in your inbox; your answer goes to the customer. Messages are deleted automatically after twelve months, as the privacy page promises.

Customer accounts

Copy link

Customers use an account to follow their orders and pay faster. Most accounts make themselves.

  • A customer's first paid order creates an account under the email they paid with, and emails them a link to set a password.
  • Customers can also make an account themselves with Create an account on the website.
  • Later orders with the same email join the same account.

The … menu on each row offers:

  • Resend the password email: for a customer who lost the email or let the link expire (it lasts 24 hours).
  • View orders: their orders only.
  • Delete account: for a customer who asks you to remove their account. They can no longer log in; their orders and invoices stay in the admin, as the law requires. Any cards they saved are removed from Stripe too.

Making an account for a customer

Press Add customer, enter their name and email, and press Add customer. They get an email with a link to set a password, and any earlier paid orders with that email join the account.

Products

Copy link

The bags for sale. Each one has a name, price, stock, photos and a description.

  • Search by name or SKU, and use the menu to show Published, Unpublished or Sold out bags.
  • Stock is how many bags you have. Available is what customers can still buy: stock minus any bags held by customers who are paying right now.
  • Click a bag's name, or use its … menu, to edit or delete it.

Adding a new bag

  1. Open Products and press New product.
  2. Fill in the Name, choose Vintage or New, and enter the Price including VAT and the Stock.
  3. Add the weight, pieces and sizes (for example 20, 60–80 pieces, mixed sizes). They make the line shown under the name: “Approx. 20 kg · 60–80 pieces · mixed sizes”.
  4. Write a one-sentence Short description and a fuller Description (condition, what is usually inside, any wear).
  5. Under Add photos, choose up to 6 photos.
  6. Leave Published on to put it in the shop straight away. Switch Featured on to show it first on the home page.
  7. Press Create product. The edit page opens: give each photo its alt text (see below) and check everything with View in shop.

What each field does

FieldWhat it is for
NameShown in the shop, the basket, on invoices and in the web address. Write it in sentence case: “Vintage denim bag”.
Web address(Edit page only.) The end of the bag's address after /shop/. Made from the name; if you change it, the old address keeps working.
KindVintage or New. Customers can filter the shop by it, and vintage bags say they are secondhand.
SKUYour own stock code. Optional; shown in the admin and on orders.
Short descriptionOne sentence for product cards and search results.
DescriptionThe full text on the bag's page. You can use simple formatting (see the formatting guide); a preview shows how it will look.
Price (includes VAT)What the customer pays for one bag, before delivery.

Weight, Pieces, SizesOptional. Together they make the line under the name.

FeaturedShown first on the home page, which shows four bags; the newest bags fill any spaces.

Photos

  • Files: JPEG, PNG or WebP, at least 600 pixels wide and up to 10 MB each. Photos straight from a phone are fine: the website turns them the right way up, makes smaller copies for fast pages, and removes hidden details such as the location.
  • Main photo: the first photo becomes the main one, shown on product cards. Press Make main photo on another to change it.
  • Order: use the up and down arrows to change the gallery order.
  • Alt text: a short description of each photo, for customers who cannot see it and for Google, for example “Rails of denim jackets in the warehouse”. It is required and saves when you click away from the box.
  • Delete: the bin icon removes a photo for good, after asking.

Good photos sell bags. Use daylight, show the bag open with a few pieces laid out, and take the main photo in portrait (taller than wide), which fits the product cards best.

Stock and sold-out bags

  • When stock reaches 0 the bag shows Sold out in the shop, with a link for customers to ask when it is back. It stays listed so people can still find it.
  • To restock, open the bag and change Stock, then press Save.
  • While a customer is paying, their bags are held for 35 minutes. If a bag looks sold out but you have stock, someone is probably paying for it right now.
  • If someone buys while you have the edit page open and you also changed Stock, the website asks you to check the number again before saving, so no sale is lost.

Removing a bag

To take a bag out of the shop for a while, switch Published off. To remove it for good, use Delete. Past orders keep their own copy of the details, so deleting a bag never changes an order or invoice. A deleted bag's web address stops working; add a redirect if it was shared widely.

Pages

Copy link

The words on the website: the home page, shop, Find us and Contact headings, and your own pages such as the legal pages.

  • Site pages (Home, Shop online, Find us, Contact us) are part of the website. You can change their title, heading and intro, but not their address, and they cannot be deleted.
  • Content pages are your own pages with their own text, such as Delivery and returns. Published pages with Show in footer appear at the bottom of every page.

Editing a page

  1. Open Pages and click the page's name.
  2. Change the Title (browser tab and search results), Heading (the big heading on the page) or Intro (the sentence under it).
  3. For content pages, edit the Body. Switch on Preview to see how it will look.
  4. Press Save, then View on the site to check it.

To add a new page, press New page, type its Address yourself (lowercase words joined by dashes, such as size-guide), write the text, switch Published on and save. New pages start unpublished.

Formatting the text

Page bodies and product descriptions use simple symbols for formatting, called Markdown:

You typeYou get
## What delivery costsA heading
- Posted to UK addresses onlyA bullet point (one line each)
1. Email usA numbered step
**free**Bold text
[Contact us](/contact)A link called “Contact us”
An empty lineA new paragraph

Words in double curly brackets, such as {{business_email}} or {{delivery_charge}}, are filled in from the settings when the page is shown, so the legal pages always match the shop settings. The full list is under Placeholders you can use in the body on the edit page.

The legal pages

Four pages were written as drafts for you to read and approve: Privacy and cookies, Terms and conditions, Delivery and returns and the Model cancellation form. Each starts with the line “Draft for the client's approval — replace before launch.”, which also shows on the live page.

  1. Read each page carefully, especially the delivery times and whether returns can be brought to the warehouse (both set in Settings → Shop).
  2. Change anything that is not right for your business.
  3. Delete the draft line at the top and press Save. The “Needs approval” label and the dashboard warning disappear.

Keep them published. An online shop must show these pages. If one is unpublished or deleted, the dashboard warns you. Deleting a page is permanent, and its address stops working.

Search engines (SEO)

Copy link

How the shop looks in Google and when a link is shared on Instagram, WhatsApp or Facebook. Admins and SEO managers use this part.

Everything works well without touching it: every page and bag gets a sensible title and description automatically. Use these tools to fine-tune.

  • Overview shows every public page and bag as Google would, with advice such as a missing description or missing photo alt text. Press Edit listing to change one.
  • Search listing (on the edit listing page, and at the bottom of product and page edit pages): a custom title and description, a share image, or hiding a page from Google. A blank field uses the default shown inside it.
  • Title lengths around 60 characters and descriptions around 155 are guides, not rules.

Redirects

A redirect sends visitors from an old address to a new one. The website adds one by itself whenever you change a bag's or a page's address. Add your own with New redirect: enter the Old path (such as /old-page) and the Destination. Use 301 permanent unless the move is temporary. A redirect only works on an address that no longer exists.

SEO settings

The defaults every page falls back to: the Site name, the Title suffix added to every page title, the Default description, the Default share image (1200 × 630 pixels works best), and the Google Search Console token if you use Search Console.

Business details and opening hours

Copy link

Settings → Business (admins only). These details appear in the footer, on invoices, in emails, on the Find us page and in Google.

  • Identity: the trading name, legal name, company number, where registered and VAT number. The law requires these on the website, emails and invoices.
  • Address and Registered office (switch on Same as the address above if they match).
  • Email: required by law, and online ordering stays closed until it is set. Shown in the footer, on the Contact page, in the legal pages and in order emails.
  • Phone, Instagram and TikTok: blank ones are simply left off the site. Instagram and TikTok need the full address, such as https://www.instagram.com/yourname/.
  • Map link: the “Get directions” link on the Find us page. Latitude / Longitude put the map pin in Google results; leave them blank if unsure.

Changing the opening hours

  1. Open Settings → Business and scroll to Opening hours.
  2. For each day, set the Opens and Closes times, or switch Closed on.
  3. Press Save. The footer, the Find us page and Google get the new hours at once. Days with the same hours are grouped automatically, such as “Mon–Fri 10am–5pm”.

Changes apply to new invoices only; invoices already issued keep the details from the day they were paid.

Delivery and ordering

Copy link

Settings → Shop (admins and shop managers): the delivery charge and times, and where new orders are emailed.

When can customers pay?

Customers can pay only when four things are set: the delivery charge, the notification email, the shop's public email (Settings → Business) and the Stripe key (the developer). Until then the basket says “Online ordering opens soon”, and the rest of the website works as normal.

The settings

SettingWhat it does

Not set yetKeeps online ordering closed until you are ready.
Delivery labelThe name of the charge, normally “UK delivery”.

Staff accounts

Copy link

Settings → Users (admins only): who can log in to the admin and what they can do.

Inviting a colleague

  1. Press Invite.
  2. Enter their Name and Email, and choose a Role: Admin, Shop manager or SEO manager (what each can do).
  3. Press Send invitation. They get an email to set their own password; the link lasts a day.

Shop emails: every staff account, whatever its role, gets each new order, a copy of each customer's order confirmation and each contact message, until an admin switches one off under Edit. An account whose email address is not confirmed yet gets none until it is. The Shop emails column shows what each person gets.

Editing a colleague

  1. Open the row's … menu and choose Edit.
  2. Change their Name, Email or Role. A new email address works straight away for logging in and for the shop emails, so let them know.
  3. Under Shop emails, switch New orders, Copies of order confirmations and Contact messages on or off for that person.
  4. Press Save.

On your own row, Edit only chooses your shop emails: you change your own name and email under Settings → Profile, and only another admin can change your role. The notification address in Settings → Shop always gets new orders and messages, whatever is switched off here.

The menu also has Resend invitation (it also works as a fresh set-password link) and Delete. You cannot delete yourself, and the website always keeps at least one admin.

Your own login

Copy link

Open the menu with your name at the bottom of the sidebar and choose Settings. There are three pages: Profile, Security and Appearance.

  • Profile: your name and email.
  • Security: change your password, switch on two-factor login, and add passkeys. The website asks for your password again before opening this page.
  • Appearance: light, dark or the same as your device.

Switching on two-factor login (recommended)

  1. Install an authenticator app on your phone, such as Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator.
  2. Open Settings → Security and press Enable 2FA.
  3. Scan the QR code with the app (or type the setup key), press Continue, enter the 6-digit code the app shows and press Confirm.
  4. Press View recovery codes and keep them somewhere safe. Each one lets you in once if you lose your phone.

Passkeys

A passkey lets you log in with your fingerprint, face or device PIN instead of a password. On Settings → Security, press Add passkey, give it a name (such as “Office laptop”) and press Register passkey.

Changing your own email address. After you save a new email on Profile, the admin stays closed to you until you confirm it. Press “Click here to re-send the verification email” on the Profile page, then open the link in the email sent to your new address.

Outside the website

Copy link

A few jobs happen in Stripe's own website, and a few belong to the developer.

In the Stripe Dashboard

  • Refunds: always made in Stripe (how).
  • Payments and payouts: Stripe pays your takings into the business bank account; the Payments and Payouts pages show what was paid and when.
  • Checking a flagged order: the order page links straight to the payment.
  • Settings: keep cards (including Apple Pay and Google Pay) as the payment methods, and leave Stripe's own email receipts off, because the website sends its own confirmation and invoice.

For the developer

  • The Stripe keys and the payment notifications (webhook) on the server.
  • The email settings, if order emails or messages stop arriving.
  • The scheduled task that releases unpaid checkouts and deletes old messages, and the storage link for photos (the dashboard warns if either stops).

Questions and problems

Copy link
A customer says they never got their confirmation email

Open the order and look at the Timeline: it shows when the confirmation was emailed. Ask the customer to check their spam folder, then use … → Resend confirmation. They can also open the invoice from their account.

A customer cannot log in

Ask them to use Forgot your password? on the login page. If their account was made with an order, they may never have set a password; you can send the link again from Customers → … → Resend the password email.

A bag shows Sold out but I still have some

Check its Stock on the product page. If the stock is right but Available is 0, customers are paying for those bags right now; the bags come back within 35 minutes if they do not pay.

The basket says “Online ordering opens soon”

Something checkout needs is missing. Open Settings → Shop: the box at the top lists exactly what.

A customer wants to cancel or return an order

Customers have 14 days after delivery to cancel. Follow your Delivery and returns page: once the bag is back (or straight away if it has not been sent), refund the payment in Stripe (how) and, if it can be sold again, raise the bag's stock.

The delivery address on an order is wrong

Addresses on orders cannot be edited, because they come from the payment. Contact the customer, write the correct address in the Admin notes, and use it on the parcel.

I marked an order fulfilled by mistake

It cannot be undone. Write a note on the order so the team knows, and dispatch it as normal when it is ready.

A customer asks us to delete their account

Go to Customers, find them, and choose … → Delete account. Their orders and invoices stay, as the law requires.

Does changing a price affect past orders?

No. Orders and invoices keep the price that was paid. The new price applies to new orders only.

I changed something but the website does not show it

Check the change was saved (no Unsaved changes label) and reload the public page. A bag also needs Published switched on, and a content page needs Published on to be seen.
